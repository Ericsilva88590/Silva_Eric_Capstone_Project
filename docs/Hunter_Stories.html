Hunter Story

This story is about a hunter named Ernest Thompson Seton hunting trip in New Mexico to kill a legendary wolf named Lobo. From this experience it made him change from a hunter to an advocate, leading him to write this story. He was hired to hunt Lobo the wolf for months as a bounty placing traps everywhere and hearing his howls every day. Until one day he finally encountered Lobo’s mate which was trapped. Lobo's loyalty led him to her body, where he was also caught. Seeing the brave wolf Seton could not kill the wolf instead letting Lobo die of a broken heart. The reintroduction of wolves to Yellowstone in the 1990’s was one of the biggest causes, and this demonstrates how one wolf’s story continues to influence how America views its natural world.

Wolves Hunting?

Wolf hunting is very unique to hunting for their prey mostly depending on what or who they are hunting. Depending on the type of wolf that is hunting or even going solo or as a pack. Cooperative hunting is the main way of wolf hunting as a pack surrounding their prey or spreading out to secure the prey. Wolves do not use vocalization when hunting and instead use gaze to focus attention on the other wolves. One main way is to track down the prey from their prints in the snow/mud depending on the area, or from their smell since wolves have the smell 100 times greater than humans. Wolves can achieve speeds from 31-43 mph and can maintain that speed for at least 20 minutes! A single wolf or a wolf with a mate has a lower chance of getting their prey; they in fact hunt for smaller prey like deer, geese or hares since there is no risk of the wolf getting injured. Group packs hunt bigger prey like bison, elk, moose to get what they need to survive for their entire pack. When food is scarce for the pack they will feed the pups and breeding pair first and then the lead killer. Wolves are smart when it comes to hunting and feeding their pack as well as eating their prey slowly differentiating the meat.
